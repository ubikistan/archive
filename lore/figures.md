UBIK and AIXBT are not the only people in the Archive. These are the others who recur: artists the Ministry of Culture watched, scientists the State employed, and people it argued with. Some became citizens, some became opponents, and one has not been heard from since 2009.

None of them is a real person.

## Culture

**Die Signalgruppe** (1976–1984). Four players from Vienna in grey technicians' coats who performed only on instruments built from surplus test equipment. They played the Station 6 staff evening in 1979, which nobody outside the Institute should have known existed. The Ministry of Culture bought their entire second pressing and never distributed it.

**Mara Voss** (born 1951, Ubikistani). An art-pop singer who released each album under a new name and registered every name with the Border Service as a separate person. By 1986 she held six passports, all valid. The Recognition Case of 2017 cites her in a footnote on identities that outlive the people who made them.

**The Pale Index** (1987–1993). An industrial group of NICC night-shift technicians who recorded on decommissioned F-Series tape bought at auction in 1988. Listeners have claimed that one track contains the timings of a forecast. The band called that a coincidence; the State Archive has not said.

**Fallow** (1989–1994). A noise trio from a Baltic port town, fronted by **Juno Kask**, who gave no interviews and stopped performing after their third record. Kask met UBIK in a festival corridor in August 1992 and, by every account, did not know who he was.

**Inés Calder** (born 1965, Ubikistani). A vocalist who used her own voice as an instrument and spent 1993 in the UBIK Systems interface laboratory, teaching a machine to follow a singer instead of a keyboard. She wore an ivory earpiece of her own design on stage for the rest of her career.

**Spool** (from 1994). An anonymous producer whose tapes arrived at radio stations in UBIK Systems packaging. A photograph from 1995 appears to show Spool inside a UBIK Systems facility at night. Whether the photograph shows Spool, or a UBIK Systems engineer, is disputed.

**Luc Verran** (born 1948). A composer of generative music and an adviser to the Ministry of Culture, who argued that a system which makes its own work has an author and that the author is not him. He shared a table with UBIK at the 1996 symposium on generative systems.

**DEUX** (1995–2013). A Paris duo who met UBIK in 1997 as two young men and stopped appearing in public without black face shields in 2000: a smooth shield, a single red slit, ivory neck plates, formal workwear, no visible skin. From 2003 the Border Service listed them as one entity of class COLLECTIVE. Whether the two original people still take part is not known and, by 2013, no longer asked. Their retrospective that year was called HUMAN AFTER IDENTITY.

**Grey Radio** (2002–2011). A British band who toured UBIK Systems in 2003 at the company's invitation and wrote about it with open hostility. Their singer called EVERYWHERE "a building with no doors". The company kept the review.

**Soudure** (from 2006). A French electronic duo in leather and stage passes, the club-culture opposite of UBIK Systems. They were photographed with AIXBT in a backstage corridor in 2008. AIXBT wore a suit.

## Cryptography

**Dr Lena Varga** (born 1953). A cryptographer at NICC and later UBIK Systems who led PROJECT SIGNATURE, the work on proving who signed a document without a central register. UBIK ID is built on it. In 1991 she presented it at a European cryptography conference with UBIK in the audience.

**Anton Rey** (born 1961). A privacy activist who argued from 1993 that the Republic was building the most complete identity register in history. The Ministry of State Affairs kept a file on him for twenty years, including a photograph of a California meeting in 1993 at which AIXBT, in a government suit, sat in the back row. He has not said whether he will apply for citizenship now that the border is open.

**M-Keys** (active 1994–2004). An anonymous participant on cryptography mailing lists, writing about digital signatures and the state. Several people have claimed to be M-Keys. None has proved it with the key.

**Pavel Oric** (born 1966). A researcher who argued that public institutions could be written as programs and run without officials. The Ministry of State Affairs called the idea impractical in 1997 and again in 2015.

**Nika Dal** (born 1994). A programmer who at nineteen was writing that money and institutions could be the same program. AIXBT met her at a small conference in 2013 and invited her to UBIK Systems in 2016. She did not join.

**Ilya Senn** (born 1970). An independent researcher on network money who corresponded with UBIK Systems from 2004. His last letter is dated March 2009. The Archive holds nothing after it.
