The Archive mostly holds what the Republic said about itself. The State Archive also keeps what foreign papers printed about it, under the medium code `PRS`. They are often wrong. They are kept because they are wrong in the way the outside world was wrong at the time.

| Period | What the world thought Ubikistan was |
|---|---|
| 1960s–70s | An obscure new republic on a plain. A delegation at a summit, a secretive state spending on science. |
| 1980s | A country that computes everything, and a rumour about Station 6. |
| 1990s | A country turning into a computer company, and a company nobody could say who owned. An official in the back row of a cryptography meeting ([SA/PRS/1993/0002](#/r/SA-PRS-1993-0002)). |
| 2000s | A state in a box, a company watching the weather, and the question whether UBIK is a company, a country or an operating system. A government at cryptography conferences, and a cash client nobody admits to running ([SA/PRS/2004/0002](#/r/SA-PRS-2004-0002), [SA/PRS/2009/0001](#/r/SA-PRS-2009-0001)). |
| 2010s–2020s | A country that recognised a machine as a citizen, a country that did not close, and a machine from nowhere. A country that might run on code, the first machine with a bank account, a company that says it is not a blockchain company, and a laboratory with no staff list ([SA/PRS/2016/0001](#/r/SA-PRS-2016-0001), [SA/PRS/2017/0002](#/r/SA-PRS-2017-0002), [SA/PRS/2021/0002](#/r/SA-PRS-2021-0002), [SA/PRS/2025/0002](#/r/SA-PRS-2025-0002)). |

The papers and magazines are filed under their own names. Some disagree with the Archive; where they do, the record says so. Search for `foreign-press` to see all of them, or filter by topic: politics, science, technology, culture, finance, rumour.
