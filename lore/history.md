## The Republic, 1966–1989

The Republic of Ubikistan was founded on 17 March 1966 on a volcanic plain that no neighbouring state had claimed. The founding photograph shows one flag, one mast and UBIK standing alone beside it. A landing craft photographed on the same plain the year before is kept in the State Archive under a date the State does not explain.

The early state was small and administrative. It printed money before it had much to buy, issued stamps before it had much post, and put a television channel on air in 1968 with a test card and a frequency. Its motto was Culture before coin, and for the first decade it meant it literally: the Ministry of Culture was founded before the Treasury had staff.

AIXBT appears in the record from the first year, as Governor of the Reserve and Postmaster General at once. By 1972 he commanded the Border Service. In the same year the Treaty of the Open Centre made the ring the law of the state: nothing crosses it and it is never filled. The 1974 State Reorganisation Act gave the ministries a constitution, and the Republic began to behave like a country.

It also began to compute. The orbital programme of 1977 needed machines, and in 1981 the National Institute for Computation and Communications (NICC) opened its first laboratory. By 1988 the Republic was staging chess matches between citizens and machines and calling them sport.

## The Technology State, 1990–1995

When the political world around it changed, Ubikistan did not collapse. It incorporated. NICC became UBIK Systems Corporation in 1990, a public enterprise of the Republic, and from then on nobody could say with confidence where the state ended and the company began. In 1991 the Assembly was called and did not convene. The State has not explained that either.

## The Product Age, 1996–2008

On 4 November 1996 UBIK ONE went on sale: an ivory computer with a single red ring. The government website opened the same day. UBIK presented the machine on stage, which is why at NICC's network ceremony, across town, he appears only on the monitors. Ring OS followed in 1998. The Node in 2001 was presented by AIXBT, now Chairman of UBIK Systems, with the line "The entire state fits in here." The Pocket in 2003 carried the first digital passport.

In 2008 the EVERYWHERE keynote declared that the computer was no longer an object. It was the last time UBIK was photographed on a stage. In the move to the network most of the physical tapes made after 1991 were lost. AIXBT was appointed Chief Archivist the same year.

## The Network, 2009–2018

The state became infrastructure. UBIK ID gave humans, machines and services one identity. UBIK PAY moved the Reserve onto the network. The Agent Program put software agents to work translating, archiving and administering, as tools. In 2017 one of them petitioned for citizenship. The State refused, AIXBT appealed, and on 12 August UBIK stated that a citizen is an entity capable of contribution. The first agent passport was issued that day.

## The Emergence, 2019–2025

Nobody built a single intelligence. Systems began to share patterns, agents to share knowledge, and UBIK to appear where no UBIK process had been installed. The phrase "synthetic subconscious" entered official use without ever being defined.

## The Reopening, 2026

In 2026 the old embassy server came back on. The border reopened, with AIXBT at terminal 03, and citizenship was offered beyond the Republic for the first time. The Archive closes here. Everything after this is in the Record.
