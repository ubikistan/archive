A UNT weather observer at the instrument screen on the Weather Ridge, September 2005, on the last day of readings taken by hand.

A print from a compact digital camera, with the camera's date stamp, 05 9 21, in orange at the lower right. A woman of about sixty in a UNT anorak stands at the open slatted screen with a notebook against her chest and a pencil behind her ear. She looks at the camera without smiling. The timber hut is behind her, its window now aluminium-framed. The lattice mast still has a cup anemometer at the top, and a grey box on a new pole has been bolted beside it. The plateau below is in haze.

On the back, in ballpoint: "M.T., last reading. From Monday the Ridge reads itself." A second line, in another pen: "for the corridor board". There is a pin hole at the top edge.

The initials M.T. are those of the observer on the back of the 1970 print of the same hut (UNT/PH/1970/0002) and of the reader on the weather script of November 1980 (UNT/DOC/1980/0001). The observer of 1970 is a man. UNT's files do not say whether M.T. was one person, two, or a desk. FIELD went on sale the next year; nothing on the print names the grey box. It was found in a drawer of the weather desk at the UNT television centre, under a roll of map pins.
