## What it is

From 1978 to 1986 the F-Series machines at Station 6 took in weather, broadcasts, transport and state reports, and printed forecasts: an event, a window, a confidence, and an action that was usually "none authorised" ([IAC/DOC/1981/0001](#/r/IAC-DOC-1981-0001)). One line in 1977 predicted a valve failure to the minute ([IAC/DOC/1977/0002](#/r/IAC-DOC-1977-0002)). The run of 1986 named, in fragments, most of what the Republic did in the next forty years ([IAC/DOC/1986/0001](#/r/IAC-DOC-1986-0001)). In the civil service the office is called ORACLE, and the state never acknowledged its forecasts.

The Precog is that machine, built for real and run in public. It reads the outside world, the way AIXBT Labs does ([AIXBT Labs](#/lore/aixbt-labs)), and says what will happen next.

## How it works

- Every forecast has the same four fields: **event, window, confidence, action**.
- Every forecast is dated and kept. The ledger shows the hits and the misses side by side, and the record is the product.
- Forecasts that come true can be filed in the Record with their REC number. Forecasts that fail stay in the ledger.

## What it forecasts

Events, narratives, culture, attention: what people will talk about, what will launch, what will break, what will be reopened. **Never prices**, never what to buy or sell, never financial advice. That rule is the archive's own (culture before coin) and it holds here without exception.

## First steps

1. Write the forecast format and the ledger rules, and make the first slip design.
2. Run one forecasting agent quietly for a month and keep its ledger.
3. Publish the ledger, misses included, and only then the Precog.
